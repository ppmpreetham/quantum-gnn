\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\frontheading{ABSTRACT}

Rover teams coordinate over low-power radio links whose quality changes from
second to second, so the graph of who can talk to whom is a moving target. This
project builds and audits a pipeline for allocating tasks over that graph. Each
cycle, a constrained quadratic unconstrained binary optimisation (QUBO) model
prunes the graph to a working subgraph that respects per-rover link caps, a
processing budget and exact connectivity of the mission rovers; a lightweight
trust-aware graph neural network scores the rovers on the pruned graph; a
greedy stage assigns the tasks; and an online trust model learns from the
outcomes. The encoding agrees with an independent re-derivation to within
$2.3\times10^{-10}$. Four annealing-family samplers find the exact optimum on
only 48 to 64\% of verifiable instances, whereas a feasibility-projected
annealer developed here is exact on all of them and matches exact
mixed-integer optima on 95\% of larger ones; an exact mixed-integer solver is,
however, about 100 times faster, so the QUBO is positioned as a
quantum-hardware encoding. In a causal closed-loop simulator, trust-aware
assignment beats a trust-only baseline by 8.1 percentage points (95\% CI
$[+4.2,+11.7]$, $p=0.016$) and random assignment by 13.8, while the QUBO
pruner, the only arm with zero constraint violations, trades 2 to 4 points of
raw success for its guarantees. The pipeline also runs in a containerised
Gazebo and ROS~2 world against live odometry, and in a purpose-built
interactive 3D engine with real-time differential-drive rovers and live paired
comparisons.

\vspace{8pt}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} QUBO, simulated annealing, graph neural networks,
trust, rover swarms, fog computing, graph pruning, task allocation, Gazebo,
ROS~2, real-time 3D simulation.
