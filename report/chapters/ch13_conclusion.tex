\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This project set out to allocate tasks in a rover fleet whose communication
graph changes every few seconds, using a constrained QUBO to prune the graph
and a trust-aware GNN to assign tasks on what remains. It delivered that
pipeline, and it delivered something more valuable than the pipeline alone:
a careful account of what each part contributes, measured against the
strongest alternatives available.

On the optimisation side, the QUBO encoding was derived cleanly, validated
against an independent re-derivation and exhaustive enumeration, and shown
on a five-rover example to reject, by construction, the connectivity traps
that a soft-score pruner would fall into. The solver audit then showed that
correctness of the encoding is not enough: four serious annealing-family
samplers solve only 48 to 64\% of verifiable instances, because the penalty
walls that make violations unprofitable also make them uncrossable. Moving
the search onto the feasible manifold removed the barrier; the projected
annealer is exact on every verifiable instance and within $1.4\times10^{-3}$
of the exact optimum on every larger one. The exact MILP baseline, finally,
is both exact and about 100 times faster, which fixes the honest role of the
QUBO as an encoding for quantum hardware rather than the best classical
solver.

On the learning side, trust-aware assignment improves mission success by
8.1 points over a trust-only baseline, with a confidence interval that
excludes zero, and by 13.8 points over random assignment, in a simulator
where success depends causally on both the assignment and the pruned
topology. Message passing adds a small margin that is not yet significant,
and a cloud-trained readout did not beat the fixed weights on the data
available.

On the systems side, the pipeline runs unchanged in a containerised Gazebo
Harmonic and ROS~2 Jazzy world against live odometry, and in an interactive
3D engine whose rovers drive with exact, provably collision-free
differential-drive kinematics, whose renderer costs a constant seven draw
calls, and whose comparisons against weaker baselines are paired and
statistically annotated.

\section{Key Take-Aways}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{A correct QUBO can still be unsolvable by bit-level annealers.}
Solver output must be checked against exact ground truth before any
conclusion is drawn about a formulation.
\item \textbf{On classical hardware, solve the constrained problem directly.}
The MILP is exact and two orders of magnitude faster; the QUBO earns its place
only as a hardware-target encoding.
\item \textbf{Guarantees have a price.} The constrained pruner is the only
arm with zero violations, but it costs 2 to 4 points of raw success at ten
rovers, and no pressure level in our sweep turned the guarantee into a success
advantage. It is the right choice when violating a constraint is
disqualifying.
\item \textbf{Trust is where the assignment gain comes from.} The trust
model, gates and delivery awareness account for most of the improvement;
message passing adds a little.
\item \textbf{Audit the audit.} Logging which constraints were actually
enforced exposed a silently relaxed budget in the project's own earlier
results, and changed its headline table.
\end{enumerate}

The project also distinguishes clearly between what it built and what it
used. The QUBO formulation, its verification, the projected annealer, the
causal simulator, the evaluation protocol, the planner node, the planner
service and the 3D engine were built in this project. The samplers, the CBC
solver, Gazebo, ROS~2 and the rendering libraries were used as published.

\section{Benefits}

The work offers three benefits beyond its own pipeline. A reusable
evaluation discipline: exact referees, repaired baselines, causal metrics,
paired statistics and relaxation bookkeeping apply to any QUBO-based graph
method. A ready hardware experiment: the exported Q matrices, with their
certified optima, are a benchmark on which quantum annealers can be compared
fairly. And a teaching and demonstration tool: the 3D engine makes constrained
optimisation, trust learning and their trade-offs visible to people who will
never read the tables.

\section{Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Moving rovers in Gazebo.} Publish velocity commands from the
planner node, using the controller of the 3D engine, so that the candidate
graph changes in the physics simulation as it does in the browser.
\item \textbf{Physical validation.} Build the four-rover ESP32 testbed and
replace the synthetic reliability and radio models with measured ones.
\item \textbf{Quantum annealing hardware.} Run the exported Q matrices on a
quantum annealer and compare against the certified optima, testing whether
transverse-field dynamics fare better against the penalty barrier.
\item \textbf{Delivery-aware utility.} The pruner's utility ranks links by
their own quality and misses links that delivery depends on; adding
path-criticality to the objective addresses the 0.67 recall directly.
\item \textbf{Scalable encodings.} Replace the dense matrix and the flow
family with a sparse or hierarchical encoding to reach hundreds of rovers.
\item \textbf{Richer trust and on-policy training.} Contextual trust
estimates and a larger exploration budget for training the readout.
\item \textbf{Statistical power.} More seeds and scenarios to resolve the
small margins, such as message passing over the no-graph readout.
\end{enumerate}
