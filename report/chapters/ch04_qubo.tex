\section{The Constrained QUBO Formulation}
\label{sec:qubo}

This chapter presents the mathematical core of our pipeline: the reduction of
dynamic edge pruning to a constrained quadratic unconstrained binary
optimisation (QUBO) problem. Each cycle of the simulation begins with a
candidate communication graph that may contain far more links than the rovers
can actively maintain. We wish to keep a small, high-quality, connected
subgraph on which the trust-aware inference of the following chapters then
operates. The QUBO formalism is attractive for this task because it accepts
both linear utility terms and pairwise interaction terms natively, because it
is the input format of quantum and quantum-inspired annealing hardware, and
because it forces us to state every modelling decision as an explicit,
auditable term in a single energy function. The price we pay is that all hard
constraints must be expressed as penalty terms, and Section~\ref{sec:penalties}
shows how we make those penalties provably dominant.

\subsection{Candidate edges and features}

Let $V = \{1, \dots, n\}$ be the set of rovers and let $E_{\mathrm{cand}}
\subseteq V \times V$ be the set of candidate edges, that is, pairs of rovers
currently within communication range. Every candidate edge
$e = (i, j) \in E_{\mathrm{cand}}$ is described by a feature vector
$\mathbf{f}_e \in [0,1]^7$ whose components are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:features}. All features are normalised to the unit interval
before the formulation module is invoked; any value outside $[0,1]$ is
rejected at construction time (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Per-edge features, all normalised to $[0,1]$.}
  \label{tab:features}
  \begin{tabular}{llp{7.2cm}}
    \toprule
    Feature & Symbol & Meaning \\
    \midrule
    Trust              & $f^{\mathrm{tr}}_e$    & EWMA trust of the endpoint pair, learned from past outcomes. \\
    Link quality       & $f^{\mathrm{lq}}_e$    & Recent packet-delivery success on this link. \\
    Battery            & $f^{\mathrm{ba}}_e$    & Minimum battery level of the two endpoints. \\
    Proximity          & $f^{\mathrm{pr}}_e$    & Normalised closeness, decreasing in Euclidean distance. \\
    Freshness          & $f^{\mathrm{fr}}_e$    & Recency of the last successful message on this link. \\
    Task criticality   & $f^{\mathrm{tc}}_e$    & Whether the link serves a mission-critical flow. \\
    Processing cost    & $f^{\mathrm{pc}}_e$    & Compute required to maintain and monitor the link. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The first six features describe desirable properties; the seventh is a cost.
We aggregate the desirable features into a scalar edge utility
\begin{equation}
  U_e \;=\; w_{\mathrm{tr}} f^{\mathrm{tr}}_e
        + w_{\mathrm{lq}} f^{\mathrm{lq}}_e
        + w_{\mathrm{ba}} f^{\mathrm{ba}}_e
        + w_{\mathrm{pr}} f^{\mathrm{pr}}_e
        + w_{\mathrm{fr}} f^{\mathrm{fr}}_e
        + w_{\mathrm{tc}} f^{\mathrm{tc}}_e,
  \label{eq:utility}
\end{equation}
with weights
\begin{equation}
  (w_{\mathrm{tr}}, w_{\mathrm{lq}}, w_{\mathrm{ba}}, w_{\mathrm{pr}},
   w_{\mathrm{fr}}, w_{\mathrm{tc}}) = (0.25, 0.25, 0.10, 0.10, 0.15, 0.15).
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The weights are validated to sum to one, so $U_e \in [0,1]$ and can be read
as the fraction of an ideal link that edge $e$ realises. Trust and link
quality dominate because they carry the causal signal for delivery success;
battery and proximity act as mild tie breakers; freshness and task
criticality sit between. Because every feature lies in $[0,1]$, no single
edge is ever worth more than about one unit of soft energy. This fact is the
basis of the penalty argument below.

\subsection{Decision variables}

Not every edge is subject to optimisation. A subset
$E_{\mathrm{hard}} \subseteq E_{\mathrm{cand}}$ of edges may be marked
\emph{hard-critical} by the mission layer, for example because a link is the
sole path to a stranded rover. Hard-critical edges are always kept and are
excluded from the variable set entirely; their utility and their contribution
to degree and budget accounting enter the energy only as constants. For each
remaining optional edge $e \in E_{\mathrm{opt}} = E_{\mathrm{cand}} \setminus
E_{\mathrm{hard}}$ we introduce one binary variable
\begin{equation}
  x_e =
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \text{if edge } e \text{ is kept},\\
    0, & \text{if edge } e \text{ is pruned}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:xvar}
\end{equation}
The full variable vector $\mathbf{x} \in \{0,1\}^{|E_{\mathrm{opt}}|}$ is the
primary output of the solver; slack and flow variables introduced below are
auxiliary and are projected away when the solution is extracted.

\subsection{Soft objective}
\label{sec:soft}

The soft part of the energy encodes two desiderata. First, we want to keep
edges of high utility while preferring a sparse graph overall. Second,
because the formulation runs every cycle in a live network, we want to
discourage gratuitous churn: pruning and re-adding the same edge in
alternating cycles costs signalling overhead and destabilises the trust
estimates that depend on link history.

Let $x^{\mathrm{prev}}_e \in \{0,1\}$ denote the decision taken for edge $e$
in the previous cycle (zero for edges seen for the first time). The soft
energy is
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{soft}}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\;
  \sum_{e \in E_{\mathrm{opt}}} \bigl(-U_e + \lambda\bigr) x_e
  \;+\; \mu \sum_{e \in E_{\mathrm{opt}}} \bigl| x_e - x^{\mathrm{prev}}_e \bigr|,
  \label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
with edge penalty $\lambda = 0.08$ and stability penalty $\mu = 0.15$. The
linear term keeps exactly those edges with $U_e > \lambda$: keeping an edge
whose utility clears the small threshold lowers the energy, and keeping a
worthless edge raises it. The second sum counts the number of flips relative
to the previous decision. Since $x_e$ and $x^{\mathrm{prev}}_e$ are binary,
$|x_e - x^{\mathrm{prev}}_e|$ is linear in $x_e$ once the constant
$x^{\mathrm{prev}}_e$ is fixed, so \eqref{eq:soft} is a purely linear
function of $\mathbf{x}$. The quadratic structure of the problem enters only
through the constraints, to which we now turn.

\subsection{Hard constraints as dominant penalties}
\label{sec:penalties}

A QUBO has no explicit constraints, so each hard requirement is encoded as a
non-negative penalty term that vanishes exactly on feasible solutions. For
the encoding to be correct, breaking a constraint must always cost more than
any possible soft gain. Inspection of \eqref{eq:soft} shows that the soft
energy contributed by a single edge is bounded in magnitude by
$1 + \lambda + \mu < 1.3$. We therefore choose penalty magnitudes of $100$
and $500$: any single violation costs at least $100$ while the entire soft
objective over a realistic instance is worth at most a few units, so no
feasible-gain trade can ever justify an infeasible configuration.
Table~\ref{tab:penalties} summarises the coefficients.

  dominate the soft objective by roughly two orders of magnitude.}
  \label{tab:penalties}
  \begin{tabular}{llr}
    \toprule
    Term & Role & Value \\
    \midrule
    Edge penalty $\lambda$        & soft, prefers fewer links            & 0.08 \\
    Stability penalty $\mu$       & soft, discourages churn              & 0.15 \\
    Degree penalty                & hard, per-node degree caps           & 100 \\
    Budget penalty                & hard, global processing budget       & 100 \\
    Flow penalty                  & hard, connectivity to the root       & 500 \\
    Flow capacity penalty         & hard, flow only on kept edges        & 500 \\
    Cost scale $s$                & budget quantisation factor           & 10 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Degree caps.}
Each rover $i$ has a degree limit $d_i^{\max}$ reflecting its radio and
compute capacity. Let $\delta(i)$ be the set of optional edges incident to
$i$ and let $k_i$ be the number of hard-critical edges at $i$. The constraint
$k_i + \sum_{e \in \delta(i)} x_e \le d_i^{\max}$ is encoded with a
non-negative integer slack $s_i \ge 0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{e \in \delta(i)} x_e + s_i \;=\; d_i^{\max} - k_i,
  \qquad
  P_{\deg} \;=\; 100 \sum_{i \in V}
  \Bigl( \sum_{e \in \delta(i)} x_e + s_i - (d_i^{\max} - k_i) \Bigr)^{\!2}.
  \label{eq:degree}
\end{equation}
The integer slack is represented in binary as
$s_i = \sum_{b=0}^{B_i - 1} 2^b y_{i,b}$ with $B_i =
\lceil \log_2 (d_i^{\max} - k_i + 1) \rceil$ slack bits
$y_{i,b} \in \{0,1\}$. Squaring the residual produces pairwise products
$x_e x_{e'}$, $x_e y_{i,b}$, and $y_{i,b} y_{i,b'}$, which is exactly the
quadratic form a QUBO accepts. The penalty vanishes only when the degree
constraint holds with the correct slack.

\paragraph{Global processing budget.}
Maintaining a link consumes processing resources, and the fleet shares a
global budget $B^{\max}$ expressed in integer cost units. Because QUBO
penalties require exact arithmetic, real-valued processing costs are
quantised: the cost of edge $e$ is $c_e = \lceil s \cdot
f^{\mathrm{pc}}_e \rceil$ with cost scale $s = 10$, so costs are integers
between $0$ and $10$. With $c_{\mathrm{hard}}$ the total cost of
hard-critical edges and an integer slack $t = \sum_b 2^b z_b \ge 0$ built
from binary slack bits $z_b$, the budget constraint becomes
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{bud}} \;=\; 100 \,
  \Bigl( \sum_{e \in E_{\mathrm{opt}}} c_e x_e + t
         - (B^{\max} - c_{\mathrm{hard}}) \Bigr)^{\!2}.
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Quantisation conservatively overestimates each cost, so a solution feasible
under the QUBO is always feasible under the true real-valued budget.

\paragraph{Connectivity by single-commodity flow.}
Sparsity and degree caps must not disconnect the network. We designate a root
rover $r \in V$ and a set $V_{\mathrm{req}} \subseteq V$ of required nodes
(every rover that must remain reachable this cycle) and demand that every
required node be connected to $r$ through kept edges. Rather than one flow
per destination, we use a single-commodity flow: the root injects
$|V_{\mathrm{req}}|$ units of flow into the network, each required node
absorbs one unit, and flow may travel only along kept edges. For every
undirected candidate edge $e = (i,j)$ we introduce two directed flow
variables $f_{ij}, f_{ji} \in \{0,1\}$ indicating whether an arc carries
flow. Flow conservation at every node $i \ne r$ reads
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j} f_{ji} - \sum_{j} f_{ij} \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    1, & i \in V_{\mathrm{req}} \setminus \{r\},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:flowcons}
\end{equation}
and at the root the net outflow must equal $|V_{\mathrm{req}}|$. Because the
right-hand sides are integers, the standard integrality property of
single-commodity flow guarantees that binary flow variables suffice: if any
feasible flow exists, an integral one exists. This is why a single commodity
is enough here. We do not ask \emph{how much} traffic each required node
receives, only whether it is reachable, and unit demands with one source make
the reachability question a single-commodity problem rather than a
multi-commodity one, keeping the variable count linear in the number of arcs.

Each constraint of \eqref{eq:flowcons} is penalised quadratically with
weight $500$:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{flow}} \;=\; 500 \sum_{i \in V}
  \Bigl( \text{inflow}_i - \text{outflow}_i - d_i \Bigr)^{\!2},
  \label{eq:flowpen}
\end{equation}
where $d_i$ is the demand defined by \eqref{eq:flowcons}. Flow must also be
forbidden on pruned edges. Since demands are unit and the total flow is
$|V_{\mathrm{req}}|$, at most $|V_{\mathrm{req}}|$ arcs can carry flow, so a
capacity slack encoding links flow to edge variables: an arc may carry flow
only if its edge is kept, enforced by a second family of quadratic penalties
with weight $500$ and per-edge integer capacity slack bits. A required node
that cannot be reached leaves its conservation residual at $1$, firing a
penalty of $500$ and rendering the configuration strictly worse than any
feasible one.

The complete energy is
\begin{equation}
  E(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}, \mathbf{f}) \;=\;
  E_{\mathrm{soft}}(\mathbf{x})
  + P_{\deg} + P_{\mathrm{bud}} + P_{\mathrm{flow}} + P_{\mathrm{cap}},
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
which the formulation module expands into an explicit upper-triangular
matrix $Q$ such that $E = \mathbf{v}^{\top} Q \mathbf{v} + \text{const}$ over
the concatenated variable vector $\mathbf{v}$. In summary, the variable
structure comprises: one binary per optional edge; $\Theta(\log d_i^{\max})$
slack bits per node for degree caps and $\Theta(\log B^{\max})$ bits for the
budget; two flow binaries per undirected edge, one per directed arc; and
capacity slack bits per edge.

\subsection{Input validation at construction}
\label{sec:validation}

The formulation module validates its inputs hard and refuses to build an
ill-posed instance rather than silently producing a meaningless QUBO. Three
families of errors raise immediately. First, feature vectors with any
component outside $[0,1]$ are rejected, as are weight vectors that do not sum
to one; the normalisation invariants of Table~\ref{tab:features} are
preconditions, not suggestions. Second, any required node that is unreachable
from the root in the full candidate graph $E_{\mathrm{cand}}$ is rejected:
no feasible pruning exists, so the instance is unsatisfiable by construction
and the caller must either relax the requirement or extend the communication
range. Third, a global budget below the cost of the cheapest spanning tree
connecting the required nodes is rejected for the same reason. Failing fast
at the formulation boundary is what allows the downstream solver audit of
the next section to attribute every infeasible sample to the solver rather
than to the encoding.

\subsection{A worked toy example}
\label{sec:toy}

We illustrate the construction on a four-rover instance, drawn in
Figure~\ref{fig:toy}. Rovers $A$ (the root), $B$, $C$, and $D$ admit five
candidate edges; the edge $(A,B)$ is marked hard-critical. Consider edge
$(B,C)$ with feature vector
\begin{equation*}
  \mathbf{f}_{BC} = (f^{\mathrm{tr}}, f^{\mathrm{lq}}, f^{\mathrm{ba}},
                     f^{\mathrm{pr}}, f^{\mathrm{fr}}, f^{\mathrm{tc}})
                  = (0.9,\, 0.8,\, 0.6,\, 0.7,\, 0.5,\, 0.0).
\end{equation*}
Its utility by \eqref{eq:utility} is
\begin{equation*}
  U_{BC} = 0.25(0.9) + 0.25(0.8) + 0.10(0.6) + 0.10(0.7)
         + 0.15(0.5) + 0.15(0.0) = 0.63,
\end{equation*}
so the linear coefficient in \eqref{eq:soft} is $-0.63 + 0.08 = -0.55$:
keeping $(B,C)$ lowers the energy and is soft-preferred. The edge $(C,D)$, by
contrast, carries stale trust and poor link quality, giving $U_{CD} = 0.06$
and a coefficient of $+0.02$: it is kept only if constraints demand it.

Suppose the degree cap of rover $C$ is $d_C^{\max} = 2$ and the budget is
tight. The optimum then keeps the chain $(A,B)$, $(B,C)$, $(C,D)$ if the
budget permits, because connectivity of the required node $D$ is worth $500$
penalty units while the edge $(C,D)$ costs only $0.02$ in soft energy and a
few quantised budget units. If the budget cannot cover the chain, the solver
routes flow through $(A,D)$ instead and prunes $(C,D)$. The qualitative
pattern is representative of larger instances: the soft objective shapes the
graph around high-trust, high-quality links, while the penalty terms silently
guarantee that every required rover stays attached to the root. The example
is illustrative; Section~\ref{sec:verification} describes how we verify that
the energy function evaluates such trades correctly.

\subsection{Verification of the energy function}
\label{sec:verification}

Every downstream conclusion in this report depends on the Q matrix encoding
the intended energy. We therefore verify the construction at three
independent levels. First, the energy function shipped in the formulation
module is cross-checked against an independent re-derivation written from
equations \eqref{eq:soft} to \eqref{eq:energy} directly over selected edge
sets, bypassing the matrix entirely; the two agree on randomly sampled
configurations to within $10^{-7}$, the scale of floating-point
accumulation. Second, on small instances we enumerate all
$2^{|E_{\mathrm{opt}}|}$ edge subsets exhaustively, evaluate each with the
independent energy, and confirm that the argmin over the matrix
representation matches the enumeration exactly. Third, we solve the QUBO
exactly by a standard linearisation, introducing one auxiliary variable per
nonzero quadratic term, with an exact integer programming solver; the
certified optimum again agrees with enumeration. The three routes, analytic
re-derivation, brute force, and exact linearised solve, share no code, so
agreement to $10^{-7}$ rules out a systematic encoding error. The solver
benchmark of later chapters, which compares annealing heuristics against
this ground truth, would be meaningless without it.

\subsection{Summary}

We have reduced dynamic edge pruning to a QUBO whose soft terms express
utility, sparsity, and temporal stability, and whose hard terms, degree caps,
a quantised global budget, and single-commodity flow connectivity, are
encoded with penalties two orders of magnitude larger than any possible soft
gain. The construction validates its inputs at the boundary, produces an
explicit quadratic matrix suitable for both classical and annealing solvers,
and is verified against independent ground truth. The next chapter describes
the solver that operates on this encoding and the audit that establishes how
close it comes to the exact optimum.
