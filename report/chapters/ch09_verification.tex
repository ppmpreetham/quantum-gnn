\chapter{Verification and Testing}
\label{ch:verify}

\section{Philosophy: a Hostile Audit}

A research pipeline is easy to make look good and hard to make trustworthy.
The verification strategy of this project was therefore written as a
\emph{hostile} audit: a suite of roughly five hundred automated checks whose
job is to break every claim the report makes. Three rules governed it.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Independent answer keys.} Wherever possible the expected value
comes from code or mathematics that shares nothing with the code under test:
a term-by-term re-derivation of the energy, a pencil-and-paper optimum, an
exact solver or exhaustive enumeration.
\item \textbf{Floors, not snapshots.} Quality checks assert minimum
performance (for example ``at least 90\% exact'') rather than exact numbers,
so a regression trips the suite while harmless numerical noise does not.
\item \textbf{Negative results stay visible.} Checks that document a
weakness, such as the sampler failure rate, are kept and reported, not
deleted when they become inconvenient.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Shared Toolbox}

Five helpers recur throughout: a generator of random connected instances with
optional \emph{tight} caps placed near feasibility; an \emph{independent
energy} that evaluates every term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:qubo:energy} directly,
without the matrix; brute force over all bit strings for tiny instances;
exact enumeration over optional-edge subsets with closed-form slacks and a
direct connectivity check, the ground truth for small instances; and the
normalised gap of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sol:gap}.

\section{Selected Checks in Depth}

\subsection{Analytic Optima}

Section C provides the strongest checks in the suite, because their expected
answers come from a formula rather than from any code. If degree caps, the
budget, connectivity and stability are all removed, every edge becomes an
independent choice: keeping it changes the energy by $-U_e+\lambda$, so the
optimum is $\sum_e\min(0,-U_e+\lambda)$. With stability restored, each edge
chooses between dropping, at cost $\eta(x_e^{t-1})^2$, and keeping, at cost
$-U_e+\lambda+\eta(1-x_e^{t-1})^2$, so the optimum is the sum of the per-edge
minima. Brute force over the full matrix must reproduce both sums to
$10^{-9}$ on twenty random instances each. A failure here while section B
passed would mean that the matrix encodes \emph{something} consistently, but
not the intended something.

\subsection{Domain Stories}

Section G encodes seven situations whose correct ending any reader can verify:
a required rover reachable only over a terrible \emph{bridge} link must keep
it (dropping it costs a 500-point flow penalty); a weak redundant path must be
pruned; of two identical links the one kept last cycle must stay
(\emph{hysteresis}); a hub with cap 2 must drop its worst leaf; ten identical
links must give a deterministic answer; a tight budget must prefer two cheap
links over one expensive one; and a chain of terrible but necessary links must
be kept whole. They are the tests a reviewer remembers.



\subsection{The Solver Audit}

Section H is built like a courtroom. Its 25 instances are small enough that
an answer key exists, and the key is verified twice: on five instances the
exact linearised solve must match enumeration to $10^{-7}$. Every sampler
receives the actual matrix, the same warm start and the same scoring. One
translation detail matters: external samplers expect the upper-triangular
convention $\sum_iQ_{ii}x_i+\sum_{i<j}Q_{ij}x_ix_j$, while our matrix is stored
in full symmetric form, so off-diagonal entries are doubled before hand-over.
Getting this wrong would benchmark a distorted problem. The section then
records the 48 to 64\% exact rates of Chapter~\ref{ch:solvers} and asserts
only a floor for the production annealer, which is excluded from the
scientific comparison.

\subsection{Certified Bounds on Large Instances}

Section J needs an answer without an answer key on its larger instances, and
it obtains one from a bound. For each edge independently, the smaller of its
keep cost and its drop cost is the best it could ever contribute; since
penalties are non-negative, the sum of these per-edge minima is a certified
lower bound $LB\le H^{*}$. Because $H^{*}\le H_{\mathrm{SA}}$, the quantity
$(H_{\mathrm{SA}}-LB)/\max(1,|LB|)$ is an \emph{upper bound on the true gap}.
If it is small, the solver is provably close to optimal without any exact
solve. The section reports this bound alongside the best-known-solution gap.

\subsection{Regressions}

Every bug found during the external review rounds is pinned by a minimal
reproduction. For example, a chain graph with connectivity constraints and no
warm start once defeated the annealer, because greedy growth cannot cross the
infeasible ``half a chain'' states; seeding from random spanning trees fixed it,
and the case is now pinned.

\section{Testing the Deployments}

The two deployments carry their own tests, written in the same spirit.

\subsubsection{3D engine motion tests} Seventeen property tests check the
mathematics of Chapter~\ref{ch:engine}: angle wrapping and short-arc
interpolation; agreement of the pose integrator with the closed-form circular
arc and its exactness for straight lines; that a right turn drives the left
wheel faster; that each wheel's contact point travels exactly its rim
distance along an arc; that wheel-speed saturation preserves curvature; that
spawn layouts never overlap and are deterministic; that over 1\,800 frames at
triple speed and up to 16 rovers no two footprints ever overlap and no rover
leaves the arena; that a dense swarm never deadlocks permanently; that
corrupted frame times and planner data are rejected; and the widest-path,
parameter-snapping, advisory and bootstrap utilities.

\subsubsection{Planner service tests} Nineteen tests check the service that
connects the 3D engine to the pipeline: parameter, position, fault and
comparison validation; the shape and consistency of a planning step; the
behaviour with no links in range, an impossible degree cap and a budget below
the cheapest tree; live tuning without restarting a mission; session
capacity and idle eviction; fault delivery to every paired arm; identical
topology for the paired baselines; the solver comparison on the same matrix;
and the HTTP round trip, including rejection of malformed requests.

\subsubsection{Equivalence of instrumentation} The planner service needed a
few read-only hooks in the simulator. Their addition was verified by running
the simulator with and without them on five scenarios, including the repair
pruners and the budget-fraction mode, and checking that every cycle's
results are identical.

\section{Reproducibility Practices}

Every reported number can be regenerated provided the seeds, instance
generators and package versions are recorded with each run, and the
experiment drivers write their results incrementally so that long sweeps can
be resumed. Comparisons between pruners and between assignment policies are
always made on identical scenarios. Finally, the suite is run in full before
any change is accepted, and optional solver backends that are not installed
cause their checks to be skipped, never silently passed.
